About Alvin

Hi, I'm Alvin.

I'm still figuring out who I am. The difference is, now I'm learning to choose for myself.

For most of my life, I believed there was a certain kind of person I was supposed to become.

Someone responsible. Someone capable. Someone who had things figured out, or at least knew where he was going.

I didn't really care how long it would take. I just wanted to make sure I was moving in the right direction.

So I planned. I organised. I tried to stay in control of things, sometimes more than I needed to. I thought that if I could make the right decisions, take enough responsibility, and keep improving myself, eventually I would become the person I was supposed to be.

But there was something I rarely questioned.

Who decided what that person should look like?

For a long time, I think I was trying to become someone who made sense to the world around me. Someone who fit the expectations I had learned to carry.

And somewhere along the way, I started losing sight of what actually made sense to me.

2026 changed a lot of that.

Not because I suddenly figured everything out. Quite the opposite, actually.

I started allowing myself to question things I had always taken for granted. What I wanted. What mattered to me. Why I was making certain decisions. And whether the life I was working so hard to build was really the life I wanted to live.

I'm beginning to understand that life is full of choices. Some work out, some don't, and some lead us somewhere we never expected.

But they're still choices we have to make, and take responsibility for.

I still want to grow, build meaningful things, and become better at what I do.

I just don't want to spend my life becoming someone else’s idea of who I should be.

I'm learning to discover who I am, rather than constantly trying to become who I'm supposed to be.

And for the first time in a while, that uncertainty feels more exciting than frightening.

01 / How I Think

I have a habit of asking one question too many.

I've always been curious about how things work. Not just technology, but people, decisions, and the little things that make something turn out differently from what we expected.

When something doesn't make sense, I find it difficult to simply leave it there.

I want to understand what's happening underneath. What caused it? What connects to what? What assumption might I be missing?

It's probably why I enjoy working with systems so much. I like taking something complicated apart, understanding the pieces, and figuring out how they fit together.

But I've also learned that this way of thinking has its limits.

For a long time, understanding something made me feel responsible for doing something about it. If I could see a problem, I wanted to solve it. If I could see something going wrong, I wanted to prevent it.

I still love solving problems.

But I'm learning that not everything needs to be fixed, and not every situation needs me to take control.

Sometimes understanding means listening. Sometimes it means accepting that someone else sees things differently.

And sometimes, it means being comfortable enough to say, "I don't know."

I'm still curious about finding answers.

I'm just becoming more interested in asking the right questions.

02 / What I Value

I'm learning that being there for others shouldn't mean forgetting to be there for myself.

I've always liked being someone people can count on.

I enjoy helping with practical things, figuring something out together, or simply being around when someone needs a hand.

And I think some of the people closest to me would recognise that part of me.

But they've also helped me realise something I wasn't always very good at understanding.

I don't have to know everything. I don't have to carry every problem. And I don't always have to be the person who has an answer.

One of the things a friend once reminded me was that it's okay to ask, to slow down, and to rely on other people.

It sounds simple.

But for someone who spent so much time trying to be capable and dependable, learning to accept help is a different kind of growth.

I still value responsibility, kindness, and keeping my word.

I still believe the way we treat people matters just as much as what we achieve.

But today, I'm also learning to give myself the same understanding I try to give others.

To accept that I have limits. That I'll make mistakes. That I can change my mind.

And that choosing myself doesn't mean I care any less about the people around me.

03 / What Drives Me

I get excited by the possibility that something could be different.

Sometimes it starts with a question that seems almost too simple.

Why does this have to work this way?

And then I start thinking.

What if we tried something else? What if we connected these two ideas? What if something complicated could actually be made simpler?

That's often how my projects begin.

Not with a perfect plan or a clear idea of what the finished product should look like, but with a question I want to explore.

ORZEN, for example, grew out of my curiosity about how technology could make everyday life easier to manage. AI Agents Studio became another way for me to explore what happens when different systems work together.

I can spend a ridiculous amount of time on an idea once it catches my attention.

Sometimes it works. Sometimes I discover that what I imagined was a lot more complicated than I expected.

And sometimes I end up with an entirely new set of questions.

But that's part of what I enjoy.

I don't want to stop learning just because I've reached a certain milestone.

There are too many things I haven't tried, too many perspectives I haven't encountered, and too many possibilities I haven't even imagined yet.

I still have ambitions.

I just want those ambitions to come from genuine curiosity and the things I care about, rather than from constantly needing to prove that I'm becoming someone.

04 / Beyond the Work

I promise I don't spend all my time thinking about systems and existential questions.

Well, maybe a little more than I should.

I love conversations. Especially the ones that begin with something completely ridiculous and somehow turn into a two-hour discussion about life.

I enjoy being around people, hearing different perspectives, laughing at things that probably aren't that funny, and occasionally getting far too excited about an idea that just popped into my head.

I can be very serious about something I care about, and then be completely unserious five minutes later.

I like helping people with things I can actually do, whether that's figuring out a problem, fixing something around the house, or just showing up when an extra pair of hands would help.

I'm also not always as organised as the systems I like building.

I forget things. I overthink sometimes. I can get carried away by an idea.

And there are still plenty of things I'm learning about myself.

But that's becoming something I appreciate rather than something I constantly feel the need to correct.

Because I don't want to look back on my life and only remember what I accomplished.

I want to remember the conversations, the unexpected adventures, the mistakes that turned into funny stories, and the people who made ordinary days feel special.

I want to keep discovering new parts of the world.

And maybe, along the way, discover a few more parts of myself.

I don't know exactly who I'll become.

But I'm a little less afraid of finding out.

And honestly?

I'm looking forward to it.
