Inside the project

The work a planner gives you

Putting a task on a calendar takes a moment. The expensive part comes later, when something moves and three other entries need a decision. I started ORZEN because I wanted to spend less time maintaining a picture of the day.

What gets to move?

I separated a fixed commitment from something flexible. A flexible task can be considered for another time. When the system needs a judgment from me, there’s a visible place for it in Attention. I can open the activity and inspect its time, duration, flexibility and obligation.

One conversation, several decisions

Zen is the interface I talk to. PELIOS is the planning layer behind it. The schedule has explicit rules about placement, so a conversational suggestion and a valid slot are two different steps in the design.

What works in this build

The Expo web build rendered the day view with a sample schedule. Opening a card exposed its timing and other editable fields. The examples below show that interface with fictional activities. They were captured without a live assistant or a weather service.

What I want to learn next

I want to test the hard cases with small, repeatable days. If I move one task, can I understand why another moved, and can I still choose a different outcome?
