Chapter 01 / Childhood, Indonesia

The Boy Who Wanted to Know

When I was younger, I wondered what kind of person I would become. The question usually frightened me. I liked too many things and couldn't imagine how I was supposed to choose one future.

I grew up in Indonesia, fascinated by how things worked. My father had a workshop, and I spent my childhood tinkering with bicycles, tools, electronics and computers. My curiosity was exciting, but I was also impulsive and sometimes crossed boundaries I should have respected.

After one particularly serious childhood mistake, my father responded by listening and helping me face the consequences rather than immediately shouting. He taught me to examine decisions from different sides. My mother showed me how much kindness and empathy could change the way people treated one another.

I didn't understand all of that at the time. I was just a kid who wanted to explore everything. Only later would I realise how much those early experiences shaped the way I think and care about people.

Chapter 02 / High School, Indonesia

Learning to Belong

When I entered a new high school, I found myself among people who didn't know me. I'd always enjoyed having friends, but now I had to learn how to build those friendships from the beginning.

I wanted to belong without pretending to be someone else. Over time I learned that having principles didn't mean expecting everyone to understand me immediately. I could listen, give people room, and still be myself.

I became fascinated by how people interacted and started enjoying organizational work. I moved between different friendship groups without paying much attention to their reputations. Years later, a friend told me I'd helped connect some of them. To me, I'd just been getting to know people.

But another question was becoming harder to ignore. I loved technology, people, and making things. I believed I could learn almost anything, yet couldn't decide what to devote my life to. As graduation approached, the future began to feel less like an adventure and more like a decision I was afraid to get wrong.

Chapter 03 / 2019, Indonesia → Germany

The First Goodbye

In 2019, I decided to study in Germany despite having little preparation. I only began learning German a few months before leaving, failed my first B1 exam in July, and passed when I tried again in October.

That November, I said goodbye to my parents at the airport. Seeing them cry made me understand, perhaps for the first time, how much we would miss one another.

I flew to Berlin and arrived in Kiel with help from a friend. A couple of days later I finally moved into my own room.

I put away my belongings, sat at the desk, and unexpectedly began to cry. Nothing particular had gone wrong. I had simply realized how much of my life was suddenly my own responsibility.

I had imagined independence as freedom and adventure. I hadn't imagined the feeling of being alone in a country where I barely understood the language. I still followed the plan I'd made, but I was about to find out how much I needed to learn.

Chapter 04 / 2019–2022, Kiel

Learning to Stand Alone

My first B2 German class in Kiel was overwhelming. I could barely follow the lessons, and my teacher suggested I move to a lower level. I understood why, but I went home frightened that I might never be able to study in Germany.

During that period I began speaking to God about how afraid and incapable I felt. It became a personal turning point in my faith, even though my circumstances didn't change overnight.

I went to the library, reviewed every lesson, translated unfamiliar words, and prepared for the next day. Later I chose to stay in Kiel and keep studying independently, with my mother's support. The COVID lockdown interrupted my plans, but it also gave me time to learn.

One day in a shared kitchen, someone spoke to me in German. I understood and answered. It was a small conversation, but I remember realizing I was finally making progress.

I completed Studienkolleg in 2022 and began university in 2023. I still struggled. The difference was that I'd begun trusting my ability to learn things that once seemed impossible.

Chapter 05 / 2023–2025

The Race to Become Someone

When I began university, I still struggled with German. Sometimes I had to learn a lecture twice: first the language, then the subject. But I'd learned to keep going, and soon I started expecting myself to push through almost anything.

I wanted to study, work, maintain friendships, build a relationship, and take on projects. During one semester I sat eleven examinations while helping with Kieler Woche. Later, I also worked at GSM. I believed that if I could organize my time, I could keep adding responsibilities.

I enjoyed being dependable. Friends came to me with problems, and sometimes I became so confident in my answers that I forgot to let them make their own decisions.

Being useful felt so important that I rarely asked what I needed myself. Eventually I began burning out, even while continuing to function.

I could tell you what my next goal was. I was much less prepared to tell you whether I was happy. I was so busy becoming someone that I'd stopped listening to myself.

Chapter 06 / October 2025

Choosing to Begin Again

In October 2025, my relationship of three years ended. I had imagined much of my future around that relationship. When it ended, I began questioning why I had been in such a hurry to become someone.

I realized how much of my sense of worth depended on being seen as capable, useful, and dependable. I could help other people with their problems, but I wasn't comfortable showing my own weaknesses.

I decided to step back from the usual pace of university and give myself time to understand who I was. One idea became important: accept what I cannot change and take responsibility for improving what I can.

I began trying to acknowledge difficult emotions instead of immediately pushing them away. I practiced admitting when I needed help. Reading Mark Manson's *The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck* also made me question which problems deserved my attention.

Nothing was instantly fixed. But I was beginning to see growth differently: not as a race to prove my worth, but as a way of caring for myself.

Chapter 07 / 2026 →

Still Becoming

I wish I could say that 2026 made everything clear. It didn't. I've learned a lot about myself, and I still catch myself falling into old habits.

I've begun understanding that empathy doesn't mean carrying everyone else's problems. I can care about someone, offer help, and still respect their right to make their own decisions. I can admit I was wrong without feeling that everything I believe has fallen apart.

Leading volunteers at PPI Jerman gave me opportunities to practice those lessons with real responsibilities. Through ORZEN and AI Agents Studio, I found my way back to the curiosity that once led me to take apart bicycles and computers.

I'm also learning to value myself beyond what I achieve or how useful I am to others. That doesn't mean I've stopped wanting to grow. It means I want to experience the growth rather than race toward a finished version of myself.

The question I've carried since childhood remains: Who will I become?

I don't know yet. And for the first time, I'm genuinely excited to find out.
