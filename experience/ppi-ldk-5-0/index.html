Before anyone arrived

We started preparing in late 2025. The weekend was meant to bring Indonesian student communities together to practise leadership and meet people from other branches. As organizing chair, I put together a volunteer operations team of 17, including me. We divided the work across the program, logistics, public relations, food, and transport.

Giving a team enough room

I considered what each person could take on alongside their studies, and who was stronger during preparation or out in the field. Our division leads had room to choose how to do the work. I asked them to flag a delay early, with a way forward. For logistics, that could be as practical as sharing the price and source of an item so someone else could take over the order.

Working with an unfinished schedule

The booklet, the materials list, and the running order depended on details that arrived at different times. We asked for short summaries first so people could keep working. We marked the assumptions we still needed to revisit and left space before the hard deadlines.

Two cars, several arrival waves

About two weeks before the event, we lost the city bus we had expected to use. We already had two vehicles and drivers held in reserve. I worked with the transport lead to group the arrivals and alternate the pickups. The station was roughly a five-minute drive each way. In the spreadsheet we allowed time for boarding, the return trip, and slippage between waves.

The weekend in Herbstein

Metro TV’s May 2026 report places the event at CVJM Feriendorf from 1 to 3 May. It counts 90 people across participants and organizers combined, from 19 PPI branches. Sessions covered communication and conflict management, leadership and interpersonal relations, and financial and resource management.

What stayed with me

The piece I keep coming back to is the handoff to the drivers. A backup only becomes useful when somebody knows where to be, whom to collect, and what to do if the timing changes. That is the kind of detail I’d make room for again.
